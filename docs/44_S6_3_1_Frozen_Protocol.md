## Audit boundary and causal questions

Documents 01, 16-18, 22, 31-43 remain authoritative. SD-08 is open: all 48 primary S6.3 queens die from accounted energy depletion by day 45.405. That accounting does not establish whether the cause is excessive egg investment, insufficient reproductive restraint, inadequate worker provisioning, encounter/absorption timing, or uncalibrated metabolic rates. A food-rich environment is not proof that nutrition reaches the queen.

Current opportunities debit 0.50 J into an egg plus 0.01 J dissipated, 20 ug crop protein and 200 ug body water. The queen has the same 2 J body capacity as workers and basal expenditure 1 nJ/ms (0.0864 J/day). An affordable opportunity only requires a positive remainder, not maintenance until a future feeding opportunity. Local adult feeding signals mainly use the inherited quarter-capacity threshold or crop protein target. These are observed code contracts, not a conclusion that one threshold must be changed.

Hypotheses to distinguish: H1 removing laying expenditure changes survival with identical starting stores; H2 actual social access, not merely accessible food, limits supply; H3 late demand/encounter and coarse absorption can leave useful crop nutrients at death; H4 current-state-dependent reproductive restraint could prevent immediate reserve exhaustion while still allowing real starvation; H5 a local anticipatory demand signal might affect worker provisioning without caste-wide orders. Only evidence-supported minimal corrections may be implemented, under a second frozen mechanism decision before changing Core behavior. No coefficient search against 90/180-day survival is allowed.

## Primary literature and limits

- [Claustral founding and body condition, DOI 10.1093/biolinnean/blad149](https://academic.oup.com/biolinnean/article/142/4/397/7342048): experimental feeding affected founding brood output. This supports investigating resource dependence; it does not calibrate an established queen's daily energy budget or a reserve threshold.
- [Pamminger et al., juvenile hormone and L. niger reproduction, DOI 10.1016/j.jinsphys.2016.09.004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27614175/): experimental hormonal manipulation supports physiological reproductive regulation. This does not justify implementing hormones or importing a solitary-insect trade-off unchanged.
- [L. niger local dissemination, DOI 10.1038/s41598-019-52019-6](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-52019-6): local exchange is spatially heterogeneous. The queenless/broodless worker experiment cannot establish queen-specific demand, protein allocation or a mandatory queen priority.

Mechanistic inferences will be identified as hypotheses. No cited study is claimed to measure the numerical fixture values used here.
