The source trees were checked out locally. R0's source uses AntWorld components and independent implementations of general ideas. ### Native reference run

AntSimulator built and launched on Windows with SFML 2.6.2 at `5383d2b3948f805af55c9f8a4587ac72ec5981d1` and Clang 23.1.5. The local build used a compatibility include for the removed libc++ generic traits for SFML's unsigned UTF storage. The upstream motion and environment headers were untouched. Desktop capture approval timed out. A native author-written probe linked against that same build invoked `AntUpdater::initialUpdate`, `AntUpdater::update`, the actual `WorldGrid` and the original depot/food functions. It used 24 fixture ants, a finite food patch beyond a wall with ways around both ends, 2,400 updates at 60 Hz, and sampled every third step. The 19,200 positions contain 18,891 successive heading changes, 15 sampled positive wall-hit counters, and 29 units credited to the depot by the final sample. Sampled chords total 38,200.13 map units. Results use map units and do not provide biological rates. The original CSV SHA-256 is `5cfedc33d33ce2dc13e3bbdc45098fabe2db7d86724bb2e3651d6143f6819131`.

The SebLague checkout is a Unity 2021.3.17f1 source project. The local environment has no compatible editor, and the repository release endpoint returned no standalone player. Its local execution is `NOT_RUN`. The design assessment above is from the original code, including the actual interaction with `PerceptionMap`, and carries no measured performance claim.

## Primary biological evidence and scope

| Study | Original assay and comparison | R0 implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Khuong et al. 2013, Methods, Figs. 1–3, Dataset S1 | `Lasius niger` workers from three colonies explored a 0.5 m canvas at five inclines. Conditions were about 26 °C and 50% relative humidity. Trajectories were sampled at 25 Hz. The archived flat reanalysis used 69 trajectories and 5,797 one-second windows. Median track-average speed was 50.643 mm/s by path or 38.133 mm/s by chord. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0076531), [AntWorld data receipt](../s6_4_preflight/BATCH2_SOURCE_VERIFICATION_RECEIPT.md) | Direct observations support directional bouts, turning and some dependence on geometry. The historical R0 travel clocks top out at 10 mm/s for unloaded brood-care moves and use 5 and 2.5 mm/s for ordinary unloaded and loaded travel. These are demonstrably slower than the open-canvas assay, with unmatched underground, load, slope and age conditions. They remain `ENGINEERING_FIXTURE`. |
| Bernadou and Fourcassié 2008, §3.1, Fig. 1 | Workers walked on flat fine/coarse sand. Reported group mean walking speeds were 26.3 and 22.1 mm/s. Turning and sinuosity depended on texture. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jinsphys.2007.12.001), [prior audit](../s6_4_preflight/BATCH2_EVIDENCE_CONFLICTS.md) | Open, unloaded ant trajectories do not identify a body clearance or an exact loaded speed underground. Substrate response is an open mechanism. |
| Oh and Cremer 2026, §§2.6, 3, archived movement rows | Video detections in flat lab nests with different geometries, brood and group sizes. The inherited audit recomputed three 24 h single-worker samples. Median gaps in detection were 2–2.5 min; detection gaps include coverage effects. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.70277), [dataset](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.16893940), [reanalysis](../m0/WORKER_ACTIVITY_TIMING.md) | Direct activity evidence, partial applicability. A lack of committed movement or a task label does not measure sleep. Rest relocation probability, site choice and the standard review clock remain fixture settings. |
| Rasse and Deneubourg 2001, pp. 435–445; Toffin et al. 2010, §§2–3, Figs. 2–5 | Worker digging groups in standardized artificial substrates gave population/volume and rate relationships, as well as substrate-dependent growth. [Rasse and Deneubourg](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1011163804217), [Toffin et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2982035/) | Evidence supports population-dependent local space demand qualitatively. A contact-scale supported floor, loaded-body sample pitch, four brood per adult unit, one transit site and a 24-waypoint outward bound are explicit engineering assumptions. Actual volume comes from committed soil edits. |
| Szabó, Nagy and Tartally 2024, pp. 398–399 and Fig. 1A–B; their 2025 diet study; a separate laboratory age cohort | The [founding feeding study](https://doi.org/10.1093/biolinnean/blad149) records pupae and workers from founding queens at 49 days. The [2025 study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-06406-x) starts with 100 young colonies, maintains 22.5 ± 0.5 °C during the feeding phase, supplies water behind cotton, and records growth and overwinter losses by diet. Its analyses exclude colonies whose queen had died. [Age and social environment cohorts](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9814961/) used lab growth until an early worker threshold, then controlled brood. | Direct support for real conditions and varying early demography. Assays do not provide a universal first-year worker count or an equivalent loss schedule. Model mortality from measured internal depletion, dose, and source cutoff is reported separately from a life table. |
| Haatanen et al. 2015, pp. 112–118, original queen and colony overwintering comparisons | The authors compare actual overwintering temperatures and report different worker and queen effects. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtherbio.2015.02.012) | R0's seasonally varying release experiment concerns food supply only. Cold response, diapause, substrate humidity and natural age mortality remain separate open mechanisms. |

Direct species observations above retain their experimental units and provenance. Neither the reference simulators nor R0's software seeds enter the species evidence set. B06 and the historical nine biological gates retain their prior statuses.
