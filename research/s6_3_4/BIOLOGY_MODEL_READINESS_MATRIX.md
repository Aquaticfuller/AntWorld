# Biology model readiness

The Evidence Atlas remains the scientific reference. Its source grading follows the reviewed scheme. **A** is a direct controlled quantitative *Lasius niger* measurement, **B** is direct target-species observation or qualitative evidence, **C** is another *Lasius* species, **D** is another ant species, **E** is wider theory, and **F** is an engineering assumption. Source evidence and the grade of an implemented coefficient are distinct. Every coefficient shown here retains its existing F / `ENGINEERING_FIXTURE` status. The storage change preserves biological facts and their timing. It makes experimental histories easier to recover.

## Follow-up source reading

- Bles et al. (2018), DOI `10.1098/rsbl.2018.0070`, full article Methods and Results §3(a), Fig. 1 and supplement DOI `10.6084/m9.figshare.c.4024462`. The selected sections describe eight broodless and queenless Brussels subcolonies of 50–500 workers. In the tracked 100-worker experiments, 101 individuals made 132 returns. Mean return times for all recorded trips were 107.18 s straight and 259.82 s bent, with unequal trip counts of 115 and 17. The first, fifth and tenth food-discovery times did not distinguish route shape in this study. These measurements separate the discovery and return endpoints for a future matched assay. The path shape, time and cohort differ from brood retrieval or real nest-shaft navigation. [Full article](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/pdf/10.1098/rsbl.2018.0070)
- Planckaert et al. (2019), B08, selected Methods and Results of the full PDF rechecked. The supplementary dataset is linked by the publisher. Its experiment supports observed contact and network endpoints for workers under its feeding regime. Quantitative queen or larval transfer remains outside that experiment. [Full article and supplement landing page](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-52019-6)
- Minter et al. (2012), DOI `10.1098/rsif.2011.0377`, selected full-text Methods and Results with CT geometries and repeated observations. The control over sediment interfaces and repeated imaging is suitable for defining future structural observables. The species is *Lasius flavus*, grade C for transfer to *L. niger*. [Full article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3262427/)

The scope retains all B01/B02 negative observations and the separation of exploratory and independent evidence. Day- and month-scale parity demonstrates preserved simulation authority. Quantitative species calibration remains **FAIL** under the last reviewed comparisons.
