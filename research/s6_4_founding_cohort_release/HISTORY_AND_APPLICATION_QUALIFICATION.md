# History residency and application qualification

## Equal-authority storage

The experiments hold inputs, event IDs, every canonical fact, both observed state CSVs, geometry edits, space cuts, state/trace hashes and RNG results fixed against the published physics-25 worlds. There are three adjacent pairs in each of two workloads for each study. The standard shaft has 591,390 facts and the limited St. Petersburg dogleg has 2,550,393. The pressure case generates 474,337,896 uncompressed canonical fact bytes. The hot `WorldEvent` vectors retain capacity for 1,040,187,392 bytes, which explains most of the approximately 1,065 MiB resident peak. The reported vector estimate excludes allocator metadata, variable allocations and the SQLite cache. The bounded mode publishes the already committed facts on 11 fixed cuts, including two fully verified restores, and releases its hot prefix only after successful SQLite publication. All three pressure runs are below the preregistered 768 MiB target and exceed the 25% reduction target. The pressure database grows to 133,235,400 bytes for 11 generations. On the first paired diagnostic the pressure process median is 60.51 s with periodic writes and 9.36 s with resident history. The latter has zero writes or reloads. In the cache follow-up the corresponding medians are 99.70 and 20.99 s during other workloads. The 114.70 s slow process remains in the archive. Repeated durable publication trades elapsed time and I/O for lower RAM and intermediate recovery points. [Before-cache and cached samples](../../experiments/s6_4_founding_cohort_release/performance/storage_diagnostics.json)

Hash-only snapshots encode the same checkpoint byte sequence directly into the digest and keep the resident checkpoint limit. Paged queries copy cold blocks, immutable sealed ranges and the hot suffix in order. The single-block decoded cache checks each stored envelope, digest and chain on access. It retains only a validated block under its SHA identity. The pressure schedule goes from 20,968 to 10,568 block decodes; the smaller schedule goes from 5,124 to 2,638. Those repeated operation counts are reproducible. Across the two independently timed batches, concurrency and disk conditions preclude attributing a wall-time improvement to this cache. ## One real midpoint write and recovery

This diagnostic runs identical work on both binaries with one save and reopen on day 75. Its block counts are identical at 210 and 495, respectively. Midpoint validation medians are 842 → 787 ms and 1,513 → 1,220 ms. Process medians are 11.35 → 10.17 s and 18.98 → 16.90 s, but adjacent process ratios are 0.826, 1.026, 0.966 and 0.764, 0.996, 0.950. Advancement varied more than the individual restore difference in some pairs. The matched repetitions leave the size of a causal release-path speedup uncertain. Peak pressure RSS stays about 1.02 GiB because the large post-save suffix is still resident. The frozen single-publication schedule and the new periodic schedule are different operating modes. [Fixed comparison](FIXED_MIDPOINT_PROTOCOL.md) [Phase receipts](../../experiments/s6_4_founding_cohort_release/performance/midpoint)

On the limited-dogleg midpoint with cache, the median paired process has 10.74 s of simulation advance, 101 ms of checkpoint encode at the save boundary, 519 ms of total save including that encoding, 1.22 s of validated restore and 3.28 s of canonical fact export. Per-process medians include 460 ms in SQLite VFS reads, 45 ms in writes and 18 ms in sync. These are nested measurements and must not be added to restore or process time. State reads, row encoding and stream writes across all daily cuts typically total a few milliseconds per phase. The raw cuts separately record construction/read, formatting, stream calls and spatial observation. Windows thread CPU here has 15.625 ms resolution. A zero reported delta on a cut is compatible with small CPU use, blocking or descheduling. [Per-cut and process receipts](../../experiments/s6_4_founding_cohort_release/performance)

Three new diagnostic cuts exceeded 20 ms. The 37.5915 ms nominal cache cut on day 145 spent 37.2616 ms inside the CSV write call; state reads took 0.0757 ms and formatting 0.0723 ms. The pressure reference cuts on days 67 and 68 took 36.4374 and 21.2819 ms, with 36.2182 and 21.0408 ms inside CSV writes. All are retained. A timed write call can include stream flush, OS I/O wait and descheduling. The instrument cannot distinguish those causes within the call. The recorded legacy 30.4863 ms cut lacks this phase breakdown. It remains an admitted hard failure. ## Release disposition

The accepted legacy application result is 22/30 and `FAIL`, including all A/A controls, archived slow rows and the 30.4863 ms hard sample. The separate v7, TD-01, TD-08 and protected-observer decisions remain attached to their originally admitted workloads. The new storage measurements do not replace any of them. The unchanged application matrix reserves three admitted sessions of 240 child runs with the original workloads, same-window A/A, ABBA/BAAB and actual SQLite save/recovery, live per-core and sensor data, original P50/P95, foreground P95/max and RSS limits. The source and binary freeze is independent from the pressure diagnostics. [Historical method and failures](../s6_4_final_core_readiness/PERFORMANCE_MEASUREMENT_QUALIFICATION.md) [Unchanged application collector](../../tools/qualify_founding_cohort_application.py)

**New qualification `INCONCLUSIVE`, 240/720 collected.** Session 1's first admission failed the original 20% system-load P95 bound at 22.27%. After the original cooldown, attempt 2 passed at 2.12% system P95 and 1.56% selected-core P95. The session completed all 240 processes and 21,840 cuts with exact historical input, authority, event count, RNG and hash identity. Its maximum enabled foreground cut is 3.3226 ms. The telemetry export rejected one append-log row with 797 columns, where the approved reader requires 472. The original collector stopped before sessions 2 and 3. The available timings are descriptive and remain attached to the actual incomplete decision. No row was removed, reclassified or used to retroactively certify the session. A small next step is to independently validate a window-aware telemetry adapter on the preserved file, requiring a complete time bracket and failing on any malformed row whose time cannot be proved outside it.
