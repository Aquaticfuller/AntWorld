## Brood detection, orientation and social recruitment

Local range and unobstructed physical access define the current perception proxy. There is no published sensory-radius estimate for this B02 placement in the data used here. Ants can recognize or contact brood locally, navigate between a discovery area and shelter, and potentially recruit nestmates. These are separable mechanisms. Lenoir reports repeated work and discusses trail recruitment as an untested hypothesis. The rotating-lane study demonstrates the combined use of orientation cues by foragers. The present candidate implements a single individual pickup-site memory and uses neither pheromone deposition nor response in care decisions. This leaves brood-specific chemical recognition and any social trail recruitment as open hypotheses.

The citation trail included the retrieval papers, Lenoir's orientation reference, and later direct tracking and foraging experiments. A target search for Lasius niger brood retrieval videos, carrier paths, contact timing and placement controls found original endpoint records and general movement datasets. No commensurate independent brood-retrieval track or round-trip series was located. Section 56 reserved the flat data before obtaining it. The extra simulator seeds test the model under new streams and placements. Every revised B02 comparison is exploratory. Source PDFs and original archives remain local. This supplement publishes references and derived factual values.
