## Paper and condition matching

Kipyatkov et al. describe individual post-flight *Lasius niger* foundresses on regularly moistened cotton in dark 40 mm dishes with no added food, inspected about once per day. The authors report timing precision of about half a day. The warm 1994 treatment has 20 Borisovka and 22 St. Petersburg founders producing some workers, across 20 and 22 independent founders respectively. Table 2 records stage-specific temperatures near 25 °C and reports durations and counts of brood individuals. The warm means for egg, larva, prepupa-plus-pupa and total development are respectively 16.7, 10.2, 14.5 and 41.1 days for Borisovka and 15.7, 10.6, 13.6 and 39.9 for St. Petersburg. Observed larval duration ranges are 7–14 and 8.5–12.5 days. The table's stage and total means use different sample counts and need not add exactly. The original count method follows the first larval quantity into subsequent pupal and worker counts. It has no per-animal identity marker at each census. The number of individual brood exposures cannot be used as the number of independent colonies. The complete first observed larval cohort never ecloses in any of the 444 worlds. No selected first-census larva is right-censored. The full development archive still contains later larvae whose observation ended before death or transformation. In the closed care-on worlds, 27 Borisovka and 16 St. Petersburg worlds reach the pupal count, partly through later larvae. No closed world reaches its original worker count. The supplied nominal arms each reach the exact worker count in 6/6 worlds. Each population has 24 workers across these matched cuts, of whom 21 entered the larval stage after the first larval census. The factorial arms reach the exact worker count in 17/24 and 21/24. Of the individuals at those cuts, 57 and 72 come from outside the first observed larval cohort. None of these selected worlds matches all four published timing ranges under either original reader.

Median egg-to-eclosion of successful first-census individuals is 41.4 and 39.9 days. The intermediate success medians reproduce the programmed 10.2/10.6-day larval minima. ## Decision and minimum research

Demonstrated simulation limitations are large early larval losses, narrow overlap of first-cohort adults under the closed water budget, and late same-count endpoints when fed later cohorts fill in for the lost first census. The closed system's missing wet substrate is a known exposure difference. It cannot assign the entire discrepancy to water. Actual living-cohort loss is too large to treat as a minor shift in the clock. The data do not identify the individual causal contributions of moisture, maternal provision, larval maintenance and observation interval. No clock or survival rule was changed to fit the endpoint.

For biological qualification, obtain per-foundress daily counts and observation times and, separately, an unused warm founding cohort with known-age brood, separately identified deaths/removals, maternal care and actual water/feeding exposure. Record measured humidity and watering intervention, source population and all censored outcomes. Freeze independent colony-level comparisons for cumulative first-workers, first-cohort survival, separate stage and count durations, and competing losses. The current model would need a separately reviewed, real water exposure or intake protocol before claiming a cotton match.

For the protected-game scope, useful qualitative engineering capability can be evaluated on its own real source and custody chain. `INSUFFICIENT` cannot alone prove failure in the fed arena. The accepted F1_GATE and formal game readiness standard still include the original timing decision. Any proposal to split an engineering-only scope from a B06-matched claim requires explicit adjudication of that gate. Current readiness is also blocked on independent grounds, so this report makes no request to replace F1_GATE.
