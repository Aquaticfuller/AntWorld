# M0 evidence and modeling boundaries

| Model feature | Primary study and measured endpoint | Role in M0 | Remaining validation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Maternal handling and worker-related changes in care | Majidifar et al. (2024), pp. 1045–1049, §§2.1–2.2 and Figs. 1–2, isolated foundresses in glass tubes and counts of observed brood care after worker manipulations. The paper reports founding and established queens separately. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2435.14536) | Direct evidence that queens provide care and that its expression changes with the social setting. M0 retains individual care leases, local contact and the H2/R1 hypotheses. | No matched individual contact-to-dose or care duration series for M0. Timed simulations have a different observation definition from the paper's scans. |
| Larva and pupa retrieval | Walsh et al. (2018), pp. 10410–10412, §§2.1–2.3 and Fig. 1, matched colony cohorts tested as an individual queen or a group of three workers. Six brood items were dispersed in a 50 mm dish. [Full article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6238135/) and [data](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.qm114cj) | Direct retrieval evidence. The paper describes a foraging box outside the nest tubes (10 × 15 × 4 cm) after workers emerged. It supplies no nest volume or egg-packing measurement. | Comparative counts and exposure times do not identify transport kinematics, capacity or distances between eggs at laying. |
| Repeated local handling and food delivery | Lenoir (1981), pp. 154–158 and 161–164, Tables 1 and 3–5. Brood retrieval and regurgitation experiments measured separately. [Author's copy](https://www.dictionnaire-amoureux-des-fourmis.fr/Publications/1981-Lenoir-Sociobiol.pdf) | Direct evidence for the distinction between moving brood and feeding it. A repeat preference and a bounded spacing rule are testable hypotheses. | Does not identify renewal time, capacity, maternal dose, juvenile hydration or the M0 arrangement preference. |
| Activity allocation | Lenoir and Ataya (1983), pp. 213–232. Marked workers from five young laboratory colonies, with repeated observations within individuals. See B3-T01 in the accepted source register for exact assay locators. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1439-0310.1983.tb00088.x) | Direct evidence of heterogeneous activity. Core program residence, measurable motion and paid work are distinct observations. | Tasks and idle labels do not quantify rest, fatigue or sleep. M0 has no new age-dependent worker policy. |
| Worker activity bouts | Oh and Cremer (2026), Sections 2.6 and 3. Single-worker video motion CSV sample and paired simulations in [the worker timing report](WORKER_ACTIVITY_TIMING.md). [Article](https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.70277) | The measured detection gaps motivate an optional shorter worker review clock under physics 31. The clock parameters are `ENGINEERING_FIXTURE`. | Camera motion and committed actions have different detection criteria. A direct estimate of the activity and task-review cadence for newly emerged workers remains open. |
| Thermal rearing and wet-cotton conditions | Kipyatkov and Lopatina (2004), pp. 166–172, 1994 Methods and Results, Tables 1–2. Foundresses from Borisovka and St. Petersburg reared under specified fixed temperatures, with wet cotton. [Article](https://doi.org/10.1023/B:JOEY.0000033808.45455.75) | B06 is development evidence. The finite local water source exercises actual ingestion and delayed absorption. | The source does not measure egg budget, humidity-dependent loss or exact individual birth, hatch and death histories. M0 is a different assay and does not re-qualify B06. |
| Cavity growth and environment | Rasse and Deneubourg (2001), pp. 435–445, experiments in controlled worker groups. Toffin et al. (2010), §§2–3 and Figs. 2–5, worker digging under constrained granular-substrate conditions. [Rasse and Deneubourg](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1011163804217), [Toffin et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2982035/) | Direct evidence that excavated volume and shape depend on context, workers and substrate. M0 records ordered material edits and actual soil custody. | Neither study identifies the field geometry of a newly mated queen's founding chamber nor calibrates M0's soil, reach or excavation clocks. |

Source grades here concern the reported endpoints. The inherited Atlas A–F and WP-6A A–D categories are retained. The authoritative source-context and Methods notes are in `research/s6_4_preflight/UNIFIED_SOURCE_CONTEXT_REGISTER.csv` and the associated Biology Atlas. The M0 design uses direct species evidence for capability, indirect evidence for environmental relevance and no transferred cross-species numerical parameters.

| New fixture | Value or rule | Unit | Classification and rationale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| World extent | 0.31 × 0.31 surface; 0.30 × 0.30 × 0.15 soil | m | `ENGINEERING_FIXTURE` / D. Sized for a local foraging arena; the visible nest is a tiny pocket within it. |
| Material and surface resolution | 0.0005 geometry quantum, 0.01 surface cell; geometry contract admits 0.00025–0.001 | m | D. Parametric material boxes are authoritative. The surface grid bounds travel and sensing. |
| Egg / larva / pupa occupancy | A common 0.75 mm exclusion width for each stage; the viewer uses different, enlarged glyphs | mm | D. Reserving space at laying avoids overlaps when a larva grows. It does not measure individual stage sizes. Existing geometry rounds the wall sweep envelope outward. |
| Local laying mask | Five by five horizontal candidate offsets at 0.75 mm pitch, excluding the center and nearest axes, clipped to 2 mm body contact and wall clearance | mm | C for finite geometric space, D for the mask and threshold. An adult body can share an occupied projection near an egg for handling. Adult-to-adult collision is outside this approximation. |
| Support and adjacency | Solid probe under candidate at body half-extent plus two material quanta. Neighbor count within 1.1 mm and a clear segment | mm | D. A finite layer with an underlying floor stands in for physical support. The probe approximates contact, friction and gravity. It accepts a bounded gap introduced by quantization. |
| Rearrangement | Existing move/handling clocks; one local 0.25 mm cardinal step that raises neighboring brood count, or moves closer to the shelter with the same count | mm | C for observed manipulation, D for the objective and movement rule. Distinct positions and swept collision are authoritative. No stacked clutch or load rotation is modeled. |
| Water and temperature | Existing fixed clocks. Wet control adds a 1,200 µg water source and caps a local sip at 25 µg; 21 °C and RH 0.8 describe the assumed setting | µg, °C, fraction | D for the source size and dose. Absorption and loss remain paid and recorded. Neither RH nor soil water grants body water. There is no evaporation, heat transport, humidity response or wet-cotton equivalence. |

Sources for the new behavior are `cpp/src/brood_space.hpp` and `cpp/src/brood_space.cpp` (placement and support), `cpp/src/long_term.cpp::execute_reproduction` (payment and birth), `cpp/src/care.cpp::resolve_care_wakes` and `execute_care_action` (timed handling and contact), `cpp/src/ecology.cpp::resolve_autonomy_wakes` (locally visible return from the brood layer), and `cpp/apps/wp6b_fixture.hpp::configure_micro_world` (environment and resources). Only opt-in MicroWorld worlds use this spatial rule. The earliest demonstration is capability evidence under a specified fixture. Independent observations are still required for quantitative species validation.
