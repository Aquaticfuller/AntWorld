# Black garden ant colony life cycle

## Growth observations

The reference case is one mated *Lasius niger* queen with a humid founding
chamber, stored maternal reserves, and no initial workers. Claustral
founders rear the first workers from their own reserves. The longer
laboratory studies supplied food after worker emergence and included winter.
The census date, water access, temperature, first feeding, and overwintering
history matter when comparing counts across colonies. [Founding experiment](https://doi.org/10.1093/biolinnean/blad149) · [Two-winter experiment](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.exger.2016.09.008)

The records support a range from tens of workers near the first winter to
hundreds in well supplied second-season examples. Mature field colonies can
exceed 10,000. First-cohort hatching probability, the exact 12- or 24-month
census in each regime, and the age of maximum population remain unresolved.
Counting first sightings of larvae and workers separately avoids treating
the ratio of unrelated stock counts as hatching or eclosion probability.

Gutiérrez et al. provide an additional longitudinal *L. niger* series.
The queen capture date was 5 July 2017. Colonies had water in cotton-stoppered
tubes, received food after the first workers, and overwintered at 10 °C for
two months. The treatments varied the ratio of protein to carbohydrate and
the frequency of feeding. The table uses the dates in the released file
and includes all four diet compositions in the twice-weekly arm. Treatment
began after the first census. The first row is consequently a pretreatment
count. Each row summarizes only colonies with recorded worker counts and
retains zeros. No missing count is imputed as death. [Methods, Table 1 and Fig. 5](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.6520) · [Raw counts](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.g79cnp5n1)

## Decline and overwintering

The Tuin 2 journal follows one founder captured on 14 July 2018. The keeper
added approximately 40 larvae and pupae from other colonies on 22 May 2019.
Around its third summer the keeper estimated about 20,000 workers, then
reported stopped pupation, worker loss and an imposed reduction to 30 workers
with the queen retained. Pupation resumed after winter and the colony reached
over 200 workers by July 2022. Its high count and recovery are useful examples
of capacity and seasonality with interventions. [Brood addition post](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier/) · [Decline and recovery posts 112 and 118](https://www.formiculture.com/topic/14111-lasius-niger-wegmier/page-6)

A separate keeper report starts with a purchased queen and 23 counted workers.
It records the queen's unexpected death and the subsequent death of remaining
workers despite continued care. The origin age and cause of death are unknown.
The frequently repeated 28¾-year queen report has inconsistent dates in its
original citation. No complete, undisturbed founding-to-extinction trajectory
with reliable serial counts was found in these sources. [Keeper report](https://www.antstore.net/forum/haltungsbericht-lasius-niger-von-suckgasse-t18582.html) · [Historical record audit](https://doi.org/10.1111/brv.70035)

Kramer et al. also measured individual survival in workers collected after
one or two winters. The authors report mean lifespans of 434 and 309 days,
respectively, in fed, queenless cohorts kept at 22 °C without hibernation.
The natal stage, experimental group and worker age are separate from the
age of the colony. The queens in the source colonies stopped laying during
the winter, with brood carried over as larvae. [Methods and Fig. 3](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.exger.2016.09.008)

Casillas-Pérez et al. monitored one-year founder cohorts across hibernation.
The [archived data listing](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.7pvmcvdtj) is readable.
Individual file requests returned HTTP 403 and the dataset download API
returned HTTP 401. No sham-group counts were reconstructed from those files.
The article and data are an additional candidate for condition-specific
growth comparison. [Article and timeline](https://doi.org/10.1111/ele.13907)

## Nest space and foraging reach

Underground void volume, the distance to a resource, and defended surface
area represent different variables. In moist sand Rasse and Deneubourg
measured mean excavated volumes of 51.7 and 147.1 cm³ for 25 and 100 workers
in one series, and 10.1 and 33.4 cm³ for the same group sizes in the December
series from a different colony. The effect of season and source colony is
confounded. In a separate field study, the 52 exploited aphid patches were
about 2.57 m from the focal nest on average. The distances describe resource
locations. These observations do not identify a defended area in square
metres. AntWorld's miniature sealed nest represents the founding view, with
room expansion driven by reachable soil and group demand and outdoor reach
evaluated through actual routes to resources. [Excavation study, Table II](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1011163804217) · [Devigne and Detrain field study, Table 2](https://www.academia.edu/22468195/Foraging_responses_of_the_aphid_tending_ant_Lasius_niger_to_spatio_temporal_changes_in_aphid_colonies_Cinara_cedri)

## Physics 45 baseline

Three software seeds used the same sealed nest at 25 °C, 80 percent relative
humidity, and with the 730-day external release reserve.
The constant warm regime does not reproduce hibernation. The release budget
is finite, and food outside does not imply food reaching the queen.

Across the three extended worlds, 90 eggs hatched, 23 pupated and 20 workers

Median adult lifetimes among observed worker deaths were 6.7, 8.6 and

All queens died of energy depletion with zero mobilizable lipid and zero
crop carbohydrate. In seed 1 the nest depot retained carbohydrate. In
seeds 2 and 3 it was empty despite abundant exterior carbohydrate. The
record distinguishes food delivery into the nest from maternal access and
oxidation. It does not by itself identify which action or eligibility check
caused each gap. In the no-exterior control seed 2 had zero committed food
deliveries. Extended supply lengthened worker persistence and still failed
to support a self-maintaining colony. The older [R0 continued-supply controls](../r0/RESULTS.md)
retained 52–54 live workers at day 730 under a different model and setup.

## Energy and allocation implications

An energy-budget model can use separate ledgers for usable energy, mobile
protein and water and make growth emerge from resource input, maintenance,
growth and reproduction. Published work develops this structure at ant-colony
scale. AntWorld can apply the same accounting to its individual transactions,
with temperature, body size and activity treated as explicit conditions.
Nest inventory is credited to an individual's usable energy after uptake.
[Shik et al., life-history energy budget](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsbl.2012.0463) · [Flight muscle resorption in founding queens](https://doi.org/10.20362/am.013003)

Kervella et al., Table 1, gives resting oxygen use at 20 °C of 6.15 µL/h per
queen and 0.99 µL/h per worker in the measured groups. Conversion assuming
20.1 J per mL oxygen gives approximately 2.97 J/day and 0.48 J/day. The
current reference fixture uses 2.34 J/day queen basal energy and 0.0864 J/day
worker basal energy, with additional costs during activity. Caste, mass,
temperature, resting conditions and early worker size limit direct transfer
of those numbers. They provide a check on the order of magnitude of adult
maintenance. [Respirometry](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jinsphys.2024.104698) · [Oxygen energy equivalent used in the prior audit](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00299234)

At 25 °C and 80 percent relative humidity, the nominal brood clocks and rates
in the fixture imply about 0.97 J and 138 µg water for maintenance through
egg, larva and pupa without delay. The initial egg has 0.408 J lipid equivalent
and 65 µg water. The larval tissue target is 85 µg protein and the egg starts
with 20 µg, so the existing 16 µg ration limit takes at least five effective
protein rations. Actual timing, reserve conversion, access and losses can
increase demand. These values describe `ENGINEERING_FIXTURE` arithmetic and
locate the bottleneck between the egg debit and completing development.
[Nominal fixture](../../cpp/apps/wp6b_fixture.hpp) · [Funding and transfer rules](../../cpp/src/founding_internal.hpp)

The next model comparison should vary maternal delivery and brood allocation
separately. A local care budget can suppress new paid laying attempts when
the queen has little reserve after her own maintenance and the needs of
already-perceived brood. The reserve horizon and care margin remain modeling
assumptions. Founding queens had fewer observed eggs in the presence of
larvae in Majidifar et al., Fig. 4a, which supports testing state dependence.
The experiment estimates egg counts, leaving the causal allocation rule and
numeric threshold open. Vary depot access and care contact with the same
food budget to test maternal delivery. Use separate comparisons for adult
expenditure and water supply. Measure actual delivery, crop uptake, brood
stage survival and worker replacement before assessing the population curve.
Compare a seasonal scenario with the two-winter source-colony trajectory at
the observed ages. [Founding and established queen experiments, Fig. 4a](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2435.14536)

## Reproduction and scope

```powershell
python -B -m tools.measure_single_nest_lifecycle
python -B -m tools.measure_single_nest_lifecycle --seed 2 --supply absent
```

Use the live executable built for source `b89f5eec` or pass a current
executable with `--core` for regression comparisons. The four runs above used
the Windows v24 executable with SHA-256
`80b1c6a0909d244461e7135cfbbc8a385509c47289178d058dbc8e340994f5f4`.
The tool prints census snapshots, count and energy receipts, terminal fact
IDs and frame hashes. The simulation and history remain in memory. All four
runs passed the native ledger check and the worker-count reconciliation.

The 22-queen counts come from `Brood_production.txt` in
[Majidifar et al.'s Dryad dataset](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.pnvx0k6wd),
SHA-256 `67610b4585e14f166c442e465857ad76c740021ce3ed4c98516faad4ba70f3d3`.
Group its tab-separated rows by `Day` and `Brood_type` and summarize `Quantity`
across `Queen`, keeping recorded zeroes. Find each queen's first positive count
of that type before summarizing dates or values. Szabó's Appendix S1 means
and sample SDs use the 30 unenriched, undisturbed queens, including zero
worker counts. Gutiérrez et al. counts use `DATA_combined.txt` from
[the companion dataset](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.g79cnp5n1), SHA-256
`69549b0325504526d15ee23f2bbb81e4303cc73cdd3c1bc574afb8e34645911b`.
Filter `species == niger` and group `num_workers` by `census_date`. Filter
`freq == 2` within each date for the twice-weekly arm. Subtract the capture
date to align the observation days. Use the [existing source receipt](../s6_4_preflight/BATCH1_SOURCE_VERIFICATION_RECEIPT.md)
for file identities and the original comparison scope. The software runs
diagnose Physics 45 and do not establish an independent biological pass.
