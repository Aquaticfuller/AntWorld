# Finite release and protected founding scope

All 48 cases complete the prospective 180-day experiment. They include the six nominal and six Profile 10 seeds in each of the two reference populations, paired with either 60 or 120 funded daily portions. The source begins with 6,000 µg carbohydrate, 3,000 µg protein and 18,000 µg water. The daily portion adds 1,500 / 750 / 4,500 µg respectively, drawn from a registered reserve. The 60-portion and 120-portion cases have the same position, initial accessible stock, model, world identity and seed. Reserve entries differ in the initial checkpoint. The last positive release occurs on exactly day 60 or 120 in every case. All 24 controls reproduce the original day-150 fact prefix, initial checkpoint, cursor and RNG. The independent reader checks each source and carry inventory and individual budget against daily states across 29,644,690 committed facts. The 48 complete archives are 381,413,394 bytes on the experiment host. Their run and original-member hash receipts are public. The last column has 120 days of follow-up after release stops in the 60-dose arm and 60 days in the 120-dose arm. Use it as evidence of actual post-release development and the time available to observe it. In the common day-180 window, switching to 60 doses changes nominal cumulative eclosions by +2 across Borisovka and 0 across St. Petersburg. Per-seed differences are 0 to +2 and −1 to +2 respectively. Both Profile 10 populations have identical paired eclosion and terminal worker counts. These are software-seed comparisons at fixed conditions. Report counts and observed paired spread rather than estimating a natural survival probability.

Stopping release leaves a potentially large finite food stock already on the surface. For the 120-dose nominal controls, 280,924 µg and 313,817 µg carbohydrate is actually delivered after day 120, summed across six worlds per population. Actual queen carbohydrate uptake after that day totals 105,779 µg and 104,128 µg, respectively. Those trajectories continue to depend on retrieval and local transfers. For the 60-dose nominal controls the day-180 surface carbohydrate is low, and St. Petersburg seed 7 exhausts all three accessible source ingredients by the final cut. It still has 92 µg carbohydrate in the nest and 73 µg in the queen's crop, a living queen and 56 living workers. Those observations are right-censored at day 180. Persistent nest protein and water do not establish sustained carbohydrate availability or survival after the horizon.

## Borisovka Profile 10

- Seeds 7 and 19 die of queen energy depletion at day 54.516, before either release budget ends, in both arms. Neither produces a pupa, a worker or a delivery. Day-180 external carbohydrate remains at 96,000 or 186,000 µg. An accessible physical route and a positive source balance cannot compensate for the absence of a worker able to retrieve food. These worlds have 31 funded eggs each and zero eclosions. In the public seed-7 witness there are zero paid maternal rations, and the day-54 queen has zero lipid, zero crop carbohydrate and 745 µg body water. The post-death cut still has 580 µg mobile protein. This is an energy death with a specific budget and absent care transfers. The record does not identify the outcome under a different care rule.
- Seed 43 has a real first worker at about day 46.067 and delivery about 3.7 minutes later. In both arms the queen dies of energy depletion at day 103.130 with 875 µg of carbohydrate in the nest. The two workers are still present at day 180. In the original public witness, fact 25765 deposits 889 µg carbohydrate 156.826 minutes before death. Fact 25798 transfers 7 µg of carbohydrate to the queen 36.826 minutes before death, and fact 25806 records the resulting 7 µg boundary uptake 27.807 minutes before death. The last day-103 cut has zero queen crop carbohydrate. The committed timeline shows a small actual terminal dose despite a larger nest stock. It cannot establish the counterfactual effect of different ingestion opportunities or doses. Extending the source release does not repair this local access failure in the paired world.
- Seeds 1, 42 and 97 retain a queen and four workers through day 180 in both arms. Their first-worker and first-delivery events are identical across the pair. The original day-150 protocol records a complete chain only for seed 1. Seeds 42 and 97 still lack the original late-eclosion endpoint despite survival and deliveries. A longer fixed observation of those worlds leaves the original day-150 gate unchanged.

These findings bound the 150-day evidence to the supplied chamber, clearance, source location, stock composition and the studied reserve and schedule envelope. The extended cases cover actual reserve exhaustion at one location. They do not test natural replacement of food, humidity, field geometry, season changes, repeated years or mature colonies. The frozen blocked, absent, ingredient-omitted and low-supply arms retain their original adverse outcomes.
