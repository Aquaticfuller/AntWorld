# Early-life validation and decision boundaries

## First feeding and usable energy

Measure the clock from observed hatch to first antennal contact, handling, positive feeding and actual uptake. Retain larvae with no contact, contact without feeding, receipt without timely uptake, death and observation stop. Count independent foundresses or collection cohorts. Larvae from one foundress are clustered. A difference in doses among survivors alone can arise from unequal opportunities and competing deaths. The R2 randomized software inputs and fixed treatment blocks estimate model behavior only.

The immediate mechanistic comparison is between the existing R1, already-owned lipid availability at a verified boundary (H1), a real local hatch prompt (H2) and both. A failure to feed still requires a separate explanation from a retained crop or energy reserve. The three paid R1 larvae are an identified delay case, with exact last dose, energy exhaustion and next unexecuted pulse. They cannot estimate the species' latency or the expected prevalence of this path. [Baseline evidence](BASELINE_RESOURCE_ACCESS_DIAGNOSIS.md)

For an independent study, record continuous protected founding observations across first hatching under measured temperature, humidity and cotton conditions, with blinded scoring of hatch and care, natural nonresponses and known loss of visual coverage. Record maternal proximity and wet contact time before a meal and use a separate matched group for stage-resolved mass and composition at fixed ages. The minimal counterfactual is whether a newly hatched, actually reachable larva experiences a higher rate of true positive first feeding than the same-age baseline under the same apparatus, with enough time-resolved observations to separate contact frequency from conditional payment. A second estimand is the delay from a measured receipt to disappearance from the received compartment or other independently measured mobilization. No numeric species constant follows from the R2 hour cap or the six-hour fixture.

## First-worker hydration and energy

Measure controlled access to water independently from ambient humidity and food composition. A matched future experiment can cross wet cotton contact with a separated but equal-humidity condition and compare known access versus no access within a specified observation window. Record actual uptake or visible drinking and body mass/water on age-labeled callows, censored workers, worker foraging and brood contact, known sucrose exposure and deaths. Supply arms must state their exact introduced materials. Published adult-scout water bouts and common-garden worker survival provide distinct endpoints and age/supply contexts. Neither yields the founding callow's quantitative hydration budget. [Source comparison](R2_EVIDENCE_AND_FORMAT.md)

If access is demonstrated through the current foraging mechanism, an additional direct drinking rule is unnecessary to explain that engineering result. If wet cotton or a direct water response is later proposed for founding, review the separate accessible finite store, perception, contact, physical access, actual dose, ordinary ledger, death ties and replay scope first. Keep the depleted and inaccessible arms. A later resource factorial with the same geometry and explicit carbohydrate could distinguish energy replacement from pure hydration after eclosion. No new default or additional authority is implied by these plans.

Use the published day-49 B03 endpoint and the B01 scan windows as development comparisons with their original condition caveats. Keep B06 daily count matching with its original thresholds and publish known-age individual durations separately. Scientific adequacy requires matching independent animal cohorts, measurement endpoints and exposure. The nine prior `BIOLOGICAL_BLOCKED` findings and the unresolved worker-age, humidity, maternal provisioning and brood care questions remain in the historical evidence.
