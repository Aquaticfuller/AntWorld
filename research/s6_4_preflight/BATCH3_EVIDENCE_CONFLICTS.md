# Batch 3 evidence conflicts and comparison limits

These records distinguish an empirical conflict, a change in endpoint, a source inconsistency and an unresolved causal claim. The Batch 1 and Batch 2 registers retain their own findings. Source IDs below resolve through the [Batch 3 register](BATCH3_SOURCE_FULLTEXT_REGISTER.csv).

## B3-C01 | Program occupancy, motion, clustering and rest

`BehaviorChanged` represents role ownership. `Rested` is a committed outcome of a rest proposal. A `Rest` program can take food or return protein and an active program can wait in place. Its energetic debit depends on the assigned role. The historical 97.6% active-role occupancy is therefore incomparable to duration of movement, quiet-bout duration or sleep. B3-T01 used short-event counts and minute scans during daytime; B3-T05 and B3-T06 counted aggregation and resting-site occupancy. Neither assay measured sleep via arousal threshold or rebound. [Task ethogram](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1439-0310.1983.tb00088.x) [Site-choice experiment](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00040-004-0739-z)

## B3-C02 | Age, current role and persistent individual differences

B3-T01 reports uneven contribution, overlapping behavioral profiles and many intermediate workers in five young colonies. B3-T09 sampled both young and old workers in bait-defined forager and nest groups and analyzed pooled molecular traits. These studies support testing age, experience and individuality separately. They give neither a transition hazard after eclosion nor evidence that a shared task lottery captures stable individual differences. Experimental young/old classes also do not establish worker morphs. [Marked-worker study](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1439-0310.1983.tb00088.x) [Age × role study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-26515-1)

## B3-C03 | Size association and size-efficiency null

B3-T02 finds a head-width difference between tuna visitors and intranidal workers. The authors describe the reversed contrast for honey in an earlier assay with different site and season. B3-T03 finds no supported relationship between within-group size variation and pupa-transport performance under forced emigration. The individual association and group regression have different denominators. Food type, location, age and stress remain candidate confounders. No source supports ordering all workers by a size-based role or efficiency multiplier. [Field study](https://doi.org/10.3390/insects11070433) [Transfer experiment](https://doi.org/10.1111/ens.12549)

## B3-C04 | Local social response and access to model variables

B3-T04 manipulates deprivation and records in-nest scout and nestmate behavior. The increasing scout trail-incidence sequence itself was nonsignificant. B3-T07 supports food- and brood-conditional recruiting behavior, including a nonsignificant sucrose brood contrast and no detected brood effect on exploration proportions at fixed food type. Antennation, exchange and responsive exits are observed. The Core uses exact stored food and nutrient compatibility, with a bounded sample, as stimulus proxies. The experiments do not give a nutrient vector, reserve fraction, encounter rank budget, or time clock for a switch. [Deprivation assay](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00040-011-0177-7) [Food × brood assay](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.anbehav.2003.10.016)

## B3-C05 | Queen care plasticity and ordinary worker task selection

B3-T08 confirms social modulation and reversibility of active brood care in queens. Its measured role, life stage, cues and observation windows cannot calibrate worker role weights, the exact-depot guard or the maternal one-quarter urgency cutoff. The frozen Batch 1 care results and B01/B02 limitations still apply. [Queen specialization experiments](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2435.14536)

## B3-C06 | Geometric authority and biological information

Half-open boxes, material precedence, spatial buckets, SDF samples, invalidation halos, coordinate tolerances and host A* are software constructs. Exact volume and sweep checks support material conservation and path feasibility. Autonomous rules use a small modeled neighborhood and personal trace with additional geometric constraints. The host route API, fixed swept body cube, cache spacing and shape quantization supply no measurements of sensory range, body pose or preference. CT scanner resolution is also a measurement convention. [Frozen mechanisms and anchors](CURRENT_CORE_MECHANISM_INVENTORY.csv)

## B3-C07 | Material, geometry and excavation mechanism

B3-N02 observes 2D shape transitions in 12/20 and 29/33 excavations, depending on group size. The remaining trials are genuine no-transition outcomes. B3-N03 changes cohesion at the same nominal gravimetric water addition. Both transitions were more frequent in the cohesive treatment, while final area among groups with both transitions had p = 0.090. Neither result identifies a humidity response or a per-contact vacancy value. Exact local faces, uniformly sampled frontiers and a finite axis trace can create connected voids. Validation of chamber sizes, branching, and variation across substrates remains open. [Group-size experiment](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0902685106) [Substrate experiment](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2010.0176)

## B3-C08 | Pellets, refill and construction feedback

B3-N04 observes location-dependent pellet pickup, deposition and responses to ant-processed construction material. Above-floor assembly is distinct from underground excavation and autonomous refill. Its spontaneous drop rate at an empty pile was extrapolated and its chemical lifetime was a fitted model parameter. B3-N05 inferred refill from CT density and location. A transaction for explicit `Fill` is a material-conserving capability. The frozen excavation policy contains no endogenous refill or pellet marking response. [Individual pellet and construction experiments](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1509829113) [Thermal CT study](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2656.14040)

## B3-C09 | Underground gradients, CT geometry and source statistics

B3-N05 separates air-facing surface and mid-depth soil temperatures. Soil water was maintained at 70% field capacity. CT-derived architecture, worker growth, drying and treatment temperatures were measured jointly, so individual stimulus use remains uncertain. The article prints dry-mass `F(2,14) = 3.06` alongside `p < 0.05`. For an ordinary omnibus F test with those degrees of freedom, `(14 / (14 + 2 × 3.06))^7 ≈ 0.079`. The figure also labels a pairwise contrast. The abstract describes the same maximal depth for medium and high, while Section 3.1 lists 17.2 and 19.3 cm and a pairwise `p < 0.05`. The exact interpretation of those contrasts remains unresolved. The restricted raw files preclude refitting here. Published means and SDs remain available as descriptions. [Article, abstract, Section 3.1 and Figs. 2–3](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2656.14040) [Restricted DataSuds files](https://doi.org/10.23708/ZJTSDI)

## B3-C10 | Group size, prior digging and developmental stage

B3-N01 shows resumed excavation following worker addition and compares groups with different prior digging/cavity experience. Its May and December initial 50-worker volumes differ, with month and natal colony confounded. The December 100-worker repetition count is two in the Methods and five in Table II. The group regressions are descriptive of the setup. B3-P01 measures survival by natal colony stage and reports no significant experimental cohort-size covariate. The groups differ in ages, prior experience and outcome. Neither study identifies two sampled contacts or four connected sites per contact. [Excavation series](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1011163804217) [Worker cohorts](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.exger.2016.09.008)

## B3-C11 | Reserve depletion and age-dependent survival

B3-P01 follows callow workers to death in a common-garden design. Early natal cohorts had higher mean lifespan and lower estimated hazard through about 400 days, with no resolved late-age difference. Accidents were censored. The Core models a deterministic zero-energy or zero-water event and explicit fixture deaths. A successful finite-resource simulation can expose an energetic bottleneck but cannot establish the empirically estimated age hazard or a caste- and environment-specific lifespan. [Worker cohort study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.exger.2016.09.008)

## B3-C12 | Dietary protein, worker survival and colony growth

B3-P02 tests artificial high-protein diets in broodless, queenless worker fragments and reports reduced survival even after short exposure. B3-P04 and B3-P11 measure worker counts, survival or winter losses in young queenright colonies under different diet regimes. B3-P04 separates the longitudinal mixed model from the change between two censuses and the non-focal congener result. B3-P11 excludes queen-dead colonies from its analyses and varies prey type, with a carbohydrate growth effect and no detected carbohydrate-diet winter-loss difference. The negative worker result and some positive colony responses can coexist. No endpoint isolates a per-action nurse weight or a bulk-protein survival coefficient. [Worker dietary survival](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2012.0051) [Colony nutrient experiment](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.6520) [Two-year diet experiment](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-06406-x)

## B3-C13 | Acute thermal response, drought, dormancy and winter survival

B3-P06 records acute thermal performance and fitted lethal temperatures in workers from one focal colony. Age and season differ between active and dormant draws. The focal resting CO₂ contrast is nonsignificant. B3-P12 uses multiple founding queens and young colonies in 165–191-day overwintering assays, with worker temperature differences, queen temperature nulls and excluded dried nests. B3-P09's desiccation survival differs with acclimation and testing conditions. Acute tolerance, winter survival and water flux are distinct estimands. None can be assigned to the fixed metabolic or hydration thresholds of all castes and life stages. [Dormancy and thermal performance](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2435.70359) [Winter survival](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtherbio.2015.02.012) [Drought study](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.243847)

## B3-C14 | Flood occupancy and colony survival

B3-P07 reports 85% plot detection before and after a natural flood. The recomputation records eight losses and eight gains in detectable plots. Bait recipes differ and species identification covers a subset of the post-flood samples. Colonies were not individually marked. Survival is the authors' interpretation of the field pattern, with plausible alternatives discussed. Bait counts, plot detection and known-colony survival are separate endpoints. [Article and linked field tables](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0152777)

## B3-C15 | Queen loss, continued worker activity and reproduction

B3-P12 reports lower worker survival associated with queen loss among retained overwintered colonies. Four queen deaths and possible common causes make this an association. B3-P08 shows a manipulated chemical cue affects worker ovary activation after queen separation. The Core stops that queen's laying on death and retains other actors. Worker male reproduction, queen replacement, social reaction to queen death and actual colony lifetime are distinct open mechanisms. [Overwintering association](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtherbio.2015.02.012) [Queen cue manipulation](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2010.0984)

## B3-C16 | Founding inputs, progression and stage-specific risk

B3-P03 measures pre-worker feeding and subsequent offspring outcomes in founders. B3-P05 measures temperature-dependent first brood with population and overwintering contrasts. B3-P10 separates soil treatments, first-brood removal and survivor-only productivity. A seeded adult, egg or brood state is a declared initial condition. Stable identity and developmental progression do not specify natural age, mortality or first-worker maturity, and counts alone cannot separate delayed development from loss or a birth deficit. [Foundress feeding](https://doi.org/10.1093/biolinnean/blad149) [Thermal first brood](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15279162/) [Founding soil challenge](https://doi.org/10.3389/fevo.2020.00076)

## B3-C17 | Provisioning schedule and environmental production

The finite reserve and pulse mechanism supplies external input on a deterministic clock. B3-P04 and B3-P11 provide observations under deliberately provisioned diets. B3-P06 reports seasonal physiological change with consistent warm housing before cold challenge. These experimental contexts support controlled resource-input scenarios and hypotheses for real seasonal physiology. They do not establish natural prey, aphid or moisture feedback, repeated depletion, a daily pulse, or the phase length and common low-rate scaling of the Core. [Controlled diets](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.6520) [Seasonal physiology](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2435.70359)

## B3-C18 | Coupled defaults and non-identifiability

The same supply, exchange, task, body and geometry controls feed multiple processes. Greater resource release or energy yield, lower burn, longer intervals, different adult task access and delayed death can partly compensate in a worker-count curve. The policy's ordinary social nurse and integrated care alternatives can both contribute `nurse_weight`. Reach, loaded box size and candidate cap affect both the decision to dig and which edits succeed. Fit-independent decomposition would need measured act times, contact opportunity and outcomes, energy and water flux, cohorts and 3D shape. Count agreement or replay invariants alone cannot choose among these parameter combinations. [Shared-parameter assessments](BATCH3_PARAMETER_EVIDENCE_REVIEW.csv)
