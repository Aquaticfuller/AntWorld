# Proposed scientific scope for Simulation Core v1.0

Status **PROPOSED FOR INDEPENDENT REVIEW**. The accepted Phase 1 baseline is `2fb9da7a602235a33ed11cc96ee54ab64b2f0f2b`. The classifications in Phase 2 are conditional on this scope. This proposal assesses scientific claims about the headless Core. Product scope, development authorization and release gates require separate decisions. [Confirmed project decisions](../../docs/01_Decision_Register.md) [Development stages and gates](../../docs/22_Development_Roadmap_and_Execution_Gates.md)

## SC-01 | Species, colony stage and observation envelope

The proposed target is a single *Lasius niger* colony, from an independently founding queen through the first worker cohorts and small, early worker groups. Founding, worker access and worker-supported life are distinct contexts. Eight- and 32-worker scenarios are useful stress contrasts from prior engineering work. Biological group sizes should follow the measured cohort and assay. The scope includes active, non-overwintering observations long enough to cover the stated behaviors, stage changes and relevant survival. Observation windows are declared by endpoint. Ninety and 180 days in archived fixtures do not define natural colony ages or performance targets.

Specify source population where available, queen stage, worker age or cohort and experience, brood stages, colony or group size, temperature and humidity, feeding schedule and substrate. Extrapolation to other populations, mature colony sizes, natural annual cycles or another species needs its own evidence. This boundary matters because founding reserves, body size, task participation and development can differ between stages and populations. [Founding and worker cohorts](CROSS_DOMAIN_CONFLICTS.md#int-c03--stage-timing-dormancy-age-and-survival)

## SC-02 | Maternal allocation and reproduction

Require plausible trajectories for measured reserve use, first oviposition, egg output, first workers and maternal condition under specified founding and worker-provisioned conditions. Include endogenous condition-dependent allocation and distinguish eggs laid, viable brood and emerging workers. An effective reserve compartment and a simple conditional laying rule may suffice if each predicts the relevant contrasts and keeps budget terms interpretable. Detailed tissue histology can constrain a reserve hypothesis without becoming a simulation subsystem. Reproductive success determines the first-worker transition and changes the available care and foraging workforce. [Maternal evidence](CROSS_DOMAIN_CONFLICTS.md#int-c02--foundress-care-reserves-provisioning-and-egg-production)

## SC-03 | Brood care, custody and retrieval

Require founding queen and early worker care, including age-appropriate access, active manipulation and physical retrieval. Compare active care using the observation window used in the selected assay. Score manipulation, pickup, transport, completed placement and successful feeding separately. The proposed scope needs care plasticity under worker access and representative transport performance. It can use effective contact history or a simple local motor if held-out observations support them. General foreign-brood acceptance, full visual orientation and every cleaning act are beyond the proposed claim. Errors here affect development and the timing of handoff to workers. [Brood care and transport evidence](CROSS_DOMAIN_CONFLICTS.md#int-c01--maternal-and-worker-care-have-distinct-observation-rules)

## SC-04 | Food, trophallaxis and physiological budgets

Include finite measured food inputs, consumption, contact-conditioned transfer, recipient uptake, body reserves, energy expenditure and water loss. Compare exposure, actual delivery, assimilation and outcomes as separate endpoints. Effective crop and body compartments, an empirically bounded uptake approximation and a simple stage- or role-conditioned cost can suffice within measured diets and microclimate. Dependence on storage, uptake timing and actual activity matters to queen survival and larval development. Mass balance verifies transactions. Measured flux, timing and condition contrasts address biological validity. [Nutrition and energy evidence](CROSS_DOMAIN_CONFLICTS.md#int-c06--program-choice-observed-activity-and-energy-expenditure)

## SC-05 | Local exploration, foraging and return

Include autonomous search, discovery, resource choice, collection, movement with a load and delivery in bounded arenas or traversable nest connections. Require plausible discovery and return outcomes with available sensory and remembered information. A calibrated effective local observation rule and a finite private route approximation can suffice in an arena where cue availability and experience are declared. Exact geometry can constrain movement. It cannot supply a behavioral oracle for unseen food or unseen routes. Discovery and delivery determine whether colony outcomes reflect access, provisioning or physiology. [Local sensing and navigation evidence](CROSS_DOMAIN_CONFLICTS.md#int-c04--local-sensing-route-learning-and-social-recruitment)

## SC-06 | Context-limited social recruitment

Include one experimentally supported scout-to-naive recruitment context within the proposed early-colony envelope. Require evidence for the chain from marking and cue availability to naive responses and a defined recruitment outcome under that context. Immediate branch choice, subsequent discovery and colony throughput have separate endpoints. An effective scalar cue field with measured behaviorally relevant age response is acceptable if it predicts the controlled contrasts and respects local information. Detailed chemistry, general cross-colony cue use, physical diffusion and universally increased food throughput are outside the required claim. A context-limited chain is proposed because colony foraging depends on social as well as private information. Independent review may choose a narrower foraging claim and defer this gate explicitly. [Recruitment and cue persistence](CROSS_DOMAIN_CONFLICTS.md#int-c04--local-sensing-route-learning-and-social-recruitment)

## SC-07 | Development and survival during the active founding period

Include time from observed egg, larval and pupal landmarks to emergence, with measured temperature and nutrition. Validate a stage model across representative active conditions if the claim spans temperatures. A single-temperature claim can use measured timing at that temperature with explicit bounds and withheld cold-condition claims. Compare stage failures and right-censored survival for the relevant queen, brood and worker cohorts. An empirical bounded hazard or an evidence-supported negligible-background-risk approximation can suffice within an observed window. Separate age, stage, exposure and depletion before interpreting deaths. Full adult lifespan tails, overwintering and multi-year demography remain deferred. Emergence and mortality jointly determine population change. [Stage timing and survival](CROSS_DOMAIN_CONFLICTS.md#int-c03--stage-timing-dormancy-age-and-survival)

## SC-08 | Worker activity, inactivity and task allocation

Include actual time use, transitions and local choice of foraging, care, excavation and inactivity during measured observation periods. Evaluate context and early-cohort differences when the claim spans them. A small set of states can suffice if observable behavior and transition statistics are appropriate and their energetic costs agree with those observations. Program membership and its debit are internal quantities. Observed inactivity, aggregated resting-site occupancy and physiological sleep are different endpoints. A detailed sleep, circadian or fatigue mechanism is outside the required claim. Activity affects both exposure to other ants and resource expenditure. [Activity and energetics evidence](CROSS_DOMAIN_CONFLICTS.md#int-c06--program-choice-observed-activity-and-energy-expenditure)

## SC-09 | Limited autonomous 3D nest construction

Include actual constrained excavation, soil transport and deposition, and coarse change of excavated volume, occupancy and connectivity in a defined substrate and initial nest. Require plausible initiation, continuation and slowing or resumption of excavation when the tested local conditions change. Simple materials, local space feedback and coarse morphology are admissible if matched group data support the outputs in the claimed envelope. Quantization, clearance, topology and mass conservation remain engineering constraints. Mature nest architecture, full pellet chemistry, a universal refilling policy and endogenous soil microclimate need separate evidence. A credible coarse nest model matters because it controls access, contact and space to perform the other behaviors. [Nest form and environment](CROSS_DOMAIN_CONFLICTS.md#int-c07--material-movement-and-nest-form)

## SC-10 | Experimental inputs and engineering evidence

Use known treatment values as scenario inputs. Geometry, food and water availability, worker and brood counts, age class, measured climate and observation schedule belong to the specific assay. An environment variable may be supplied and held constant without asserting a predictive environmental response. Temperature dependence within an admitted active regime belongs to SC-07. The soil and microclimate envelope bounds SC-09. Observability, stable identity, scheduling, event ordering, accounting, persistence and spatial correctness support interpretable comparisons. These checks are evaluated separately from scientific fit. [Source and configuration traceability](SCIENTIFIC_INTEGRATION_REPORT.md#how-to-trace-a-record)

## SC-11 | Deferred biological extensions

The proposed default scope excludes pathogens and immune dynamics, natural disasters, full sexual caste and mating cycles, multi-year weather and resource ecology, overwintering, and mature or multi-colony ecological claims. Those phenomena can change survival, task choice, resources and nest structure. Use exposures within the tested scope and declare where a perturbation would invalidate the comparison. Natural prey and honeydew renewal, chemical signals outside the tested recruitment contexts and detailed underground heat and moisture transport can be valuable extensions. Existing environmental inputs and fixture controls do not establish them. Narrower claims preserve interpretability when evidence for an omitted process is unavailable. [Open scientific issues](../../docs/08_Open_Questions.md)

## SC-12 | Evidence needed to support a scoped claim

Use outcome, treatment, life stage, time origin, unit and sampling window that match the biological claim. An observed act, a count, a conditional probability, a transfer dose and an ecological outcome each support different inferences. Compare distributions or predeclared contrasts, include censoring and uncertainty, and derive an admissible error bound from the observation process or intended scientific use before evaluating held-out evidence. A nonsignificant difference alone does not establish equivalence. Use independent biological queens, groups or source colonies as supported by the assay. Repeated events, repeated census points, aliases of the same dataset and simulator seeds do not create independent biological replicates.

A bounded simplification needs evidence that the claimed endpoints remain acceptable across plausible settings and that a compensating change elsewhere is not required to obtain the result. A mismatch on a required matched endpoint, an unmeasured required outcome, an unsupported causal claim or an unresolved dependence on arbitrarily chosen settings keeps the corresponding proposed blocker open. The exact margins, dataset partitions and validation designs belong to Phase 3. Phase 2 specifies the endpoints, scope and evidence needed for independent review.
