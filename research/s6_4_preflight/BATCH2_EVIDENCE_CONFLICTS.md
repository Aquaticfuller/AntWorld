# Batch 2 evidence conflicts and interpretation limits

The identifiers below join to the [mechanism and transition review](BATCH2_MECHANISM_EVIDENCE_REVIEW.csv) and [parameter review](BATCH2_PARAMETER_EVIDENCE_REVIEW.csv). Source IDs resolve to the full article locations, assay populations, endpoints and uncertainty in the [source register](BATCH2_SOURCE_FULLTEXT_REGISTER.csv). A rule conflict refers to the audited source and a specified experimental context. A difference of endpoint or an untested extension is recorded separately.

## B2-C01 | A local resource query is stronger than the measured sensory input

`SEN-02`, `FOR-03` and the two corresponding transitions use reachable cells. After a bounded series of virtual moves, `sense` can see exact food existence and first-edge guidance. Checking each virtual edge against obstacles limits reach. It does not require an unobstructed line from the actual animal or an approach to a real source. In Detrain and Prieur (F04, §§2–3, Tables 1–2), antennal contact and drinking were separately scored at a provided feeder. The fitted sucrose threshold is a concentration for acceptance after contact. Portha et al. (F08, p. 116 and Fig. 1) excluded scouts that failed to find the drop or fell from the bridge. Neither design identifies a 20 mm search radius, hidden-patch detection probability or simultaneous exact shortest path cue. This is an unvalidated information affordance, with no evidence here of a global food scan. [F04](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jinsphys.2014.03.010), [F08](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.anbehav.2003.10.016).

## B2-C02 | Exact colony ownership excludes cross-colony cue use

`SEN-03` and `SEN-03-T01` read `(actor.colony, cell)` only. In the aged-trail assay, Beckers et al. (P15, pp. 752–756) used donor-marked cardboard with a different receiving colony, replaced the blank arm, alternated the side and removed a worker after its choice. Those choices supported a behavioral response and aging fit. That context conflicts with a universal same-colony gate. It does not show equal responses to all conspecific colonies or all chemical mixtures in nature. Devigne and Detrain (F10) tested area marks and encounter behavior, a separate signal. The congener assay from Popp et al. concerns contact and is likewise separate. [P15](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01201674), [F10](https://doi.org/10.1007/PL00012659).

## B2-C03 | Visual direction, private memory and software geometry are distinct

Carthy (F02, pp. 291–294, Figs. 12–14 and Table II) found seven of 12 trained workers with significant individual white/red contrasts, with a pooled null. The trained larval return assay limits generalization. Evison et al. (P07, pp. 263–266, Fig. 5) found less paper following in a changed room. Bonavita et al. (F03, Figs. 2–3 and S1) report bearing-dependent paths and different turning evidence in white and red light. None proves that an ant remembers exact cell coordinates, uses a vertical-distance heuristic, or recomputes future geometry. The connector solves a physically constrained local navigation problem for the program. Its saved waypoints and a forager's path trace do not implement a visual or idiothetic sensor. [F02](https://doi.org/10.1163/156853951X00304), [P07](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00265-008-0657-6), [F03](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0327957).

## B2-C04 | Contact, ingestion, loading and unloading have different endpoints

The forager makes one `CollectFood` transfer into external cargo. It prioritizes return with any cargo, and `DeliverFood` credits the whole load at a single depot. In Mailleux et al. (F05, Tables 1–2), 63 scouts that found both drops drank from the second drop on their homeward leg, with a mean second intake of 0.28 µL. The observed first drinking bout averaged 51 s. This is evidence against universally ending all acquisition after the first positive pickup. It also shows why a duration for external transfer and a liquid drinking time cannot be fitted interchangeably. Portha et al. (F08, Table 1, Fig. 2) distinguish food type and brood effects, including the null of brood on the exploration fraction within food type. Mailleux et al. (P09, Tables 2–3) measured scout unloading through nest contacts. Neither an ingested µL nor a within-nest trophallaxis is the code's deterministic external cargo quantity. [F05](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.crvi.2008.10.005), [F08](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.anbehav.2003.10.016), [P09](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00040-011-0177-7).

## B2-C05 | Directional persistence is context dependent

Khuong et al. (F01, Figs. 1–5 and Dataset S1) show curved paths and incline effects. The same horizontal data had little camera-axis bias, but a reanalysis finds structure relative to the release origin. New paired light trials from Bonavita et al. (F03, Figs. 2–3) separate old and new observations. They do not identify how origin direction is perceived. Forster et al. (P01, Figs. 2–3) show that geometry changes branch preference under equal marking and that graded asymmetry was not significant in the one-marked-arm assay. Bernadou and Fourcassié (F09, Fig. 1 and §3.1) report texture-dependent realized speed and turning. Neither speed nor direction can be calibrated from just four choices, a visited-cell penalty and a fixed straight bonus. The 10 mm/s cap is below the F09 group means of 26.3 and 22.1 mm/s on fine and coarse sand. This is a scale mismatch for those unloaded movement endpoints, with no corresponding biological maximum inferred. Koch and Czaczkes (P13, Fig. 1) found no repeatable trail-ignoring subgroup under their tested conditions. [F01](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0076531), [F03](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0327957), [P01](https://doi.org/10.1111/eth.12248), [F09](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jinsphys.2007.12.001), [P13](https://doi.org/10.1111/een.12995).

## B2-C06 | Quick route learning can coexist with errors and context-dependent cue use

In Grüter et al. (F06, Figs. 2–3), 74.6% chose correctly after one visit and 95.3% after three. A remembered arm often won against a conflicting trail, but the preference was probabilistic. Czaczkes et al. (F07, Figs. 2–4 and Tables A1–A2) found distinct error rates on repeating/alternating routes and a positive final unmarked comparison after trail experience. Czaczkes et al. (P03, Fig. 2, S1 File and S1 Table) did not recover a benefit for learning carried into the common unmarked final test (`p=0.65`). The experiments differ in colony sample and design. Immediate guidance while a cue is present and retained learning after removing it are separate comparisons. A perfectly replayed route snapshot, deterministic precedence and abrupt five-minute expiry are source rules, without measured equivalents in these studies. [F06](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00265-010-1020-2), [F07](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.076570), [P03](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0149720).

## B2-C07 | Production, dose, persistence and diffusion cannot share a proxy calibration

- Bestmann et al. (P10, pp. 795–796, Fig. 1 and Table 2) identify a hindgut component and report activity at 500 pg per synthetic trail. This is neither a natural dose per marking act nor a threshold estimate. EAG and following are separate endpoints. Von Thienen et al. (P16, Figs. 2 and 4) estimate choice curves and lapse with normalized extract per length. This different dose scale cannot convert the 1-unit increment, 16-unit cap or 0.001-unit cutoff to chemical quantities. [P10](https://doi.org/10.1002/anie.199207951), [P16](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00265-014-1770-3).
- Beckers et al. (P08, Figs. 2–4) recorded some outbound marks by experienced workers. P05 reports marking changes with traffic and contacts. P06 reports dependence on prior marks and runway position, with no significant marking increase after a wrong initial turn in that setup. P15 reports 43% more observed acts per passage for the richer sucrose treatment. These context-dependent acts conflict with a universal fixed-dose loaded-step interpretation. All are counts or probabilities of acts. [P08](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01240531), [P05](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2012.1009), [P06](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00040-024-00995-y), [P15](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01201674).
- P15 (p. 756, Eq. 3–5) assumes exponential decay and fits a 47 min mean lifetime (`SE 9.5 min`). For that model, `t_half = tau × ln(2)`, giving 1954.7 s with transformed SE 395.1 s, roughly 32.6 times the 60 s fixture. This fit is to choice on aged paper. P07 (Fig. 3) reports 20–24 h to control-level behavior for 500 passages, a different measure with possible cue reinforcement and room effects. P08 (Fig. 2 caption) uses a 1000 s lifetime inside its simulation. These are three different quantities. [P15](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01201674), [P07](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00265-008-0657-6), [P08](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01240531).
- `PHE-02` has independent tile decay, no spatial spreading, no advection and no substrate-dependent diffusion coefficient. No reviewed focal assay identifies a diffusion coefficient or a calibrated chemical mass-versus-time law applicable to this field. The repository's removed-unit counter combines decayed values and cutoff loss.

## B2-C08 | Following on the surface omits important recruitment stages

P09 (Fig. 1 and Tables 1–3) measures nestmate exits and food-recipient relays after one scout. In the no-contact category, exit probability rose from 0.38 to 0.72 with deprivation. Four/eight-day receivers sometimes laid marks without ever seeing the source. The authors infer chemical stimulation of exits. Their study does not isolate the exact dose or an activation threshold. The current policy enrolls eligible foragers independently of trail detection in the nest, bypasses connector deposition and restricts surface deposition to loaded cross-tile moves. F10 also observed food-free collective exploration and weak branch selection, distinct from food recruitment. A marked arm followed by an already active forager does not test worker mobilization or relaying. [P09](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00040-011-0177-7), [F10](https://doi.org/10.1007/PL00012659).

## B2-C09 | Cue following, correction, speed and causal response differ

P02 (Figs. 1–2) found more U-turns on leaving a marked strip and a speed/straightness benefit for experienced workers on marked paper. P04 (Figs. 2–5 and workbook) found more time near an extract line, no significant speed or straightness benefit there, and 348/398 marked-arm choices in a separate maze. The arena movement and forced choice assays have distinct choice sets. No test establishes the exact sole-predecessor gate or a chemical arrow toward food. The `FollowTrail` state reports a chosen edge bearing signal. It can be assigned with zero response weight, so paired response and blind comparisons are needed for attribution. [P02](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsbl.2011.0067), [P04](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00040-026-01106-9).

## B2-C10 | Contact-mediated activity does not imply tactile transfer of a target turn

P09 examined exit behavior with and without prior contacts in a nest. P12 (Figs. 1–2 and 4) observed an initial apparent direction effect in a less controlled assay. After eliminating trail contamination, neither initial nor final fork choice improved significantly after contact with an informed worker. The L. neglectus check (P12-C, Fig. 3) supports that assay design as congener evidence only. The null is specific to transmission of branch direction. It does not establish no effects of nearby workers, other contact channels or food solicitation. Code omits peer signals from the foraging selector. That does not by itself validate the whole cue inventory. [P09](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00040-011-0177-7), [P12 and P12-C](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00040-017-0583-6).

## B2-C11 | Colony occupancy and simulated local performance do not settle social benefit

P11 (Figs. 2–3) found uneven visitation of equidistant feeders on routes that differed in learnability. P15 (Table I) found choice changed with sugar contrast, including 50% selection of the 1 M feeder against 0.5 M in 12 trials. Group traffic combines route discovery, re-visits, departure, quality and recruitment. In the preserved S2.2 model experiments the fresh single-scout naive-discovery contrast was +2.34375 percentage points with paired 95% interval `[-8.59375, 12.5]`. Initial consecutive following averaged 2.1035 straight and 2.0459 bent steps, with departure in all 24-move trials. These are simulator results. They are neither fitted field endpoints nor estimates of natural worker or colony uncertainty. SD-01 and SD-02 remain open. [P11](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.anbehav.2015.03.017), [P15](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01201674), [S2.2 report](../../S2_2_RESULTS.md), [scientific debt](../../docs/08_Open_Questions.md).
