# Unresolved biological gaps and acceptance observations

Every root below remains open under the [proposed scope](S6_4_PHASE2_READINESS_SCOPE.md). The [blocker register](S6_4_PHASE2_BLOCKER_REGISTER.csv) gives original source-context citations, accepted cross-domain conflicts, linked mechanisms, current parameter references, additional evidence, admissible approximations and failure conditions. The measurements here come from the accepted Phase 1 evidence or the cited historical simulation records. Numerical acceptance margins and independent dataset partitions need a later decision.

## BR-01 | Founding reserves and reproductive allocation

B01 Fig. 4a found that larval presence reduced egg counts for founding queens and increased them for established queens under the respective manipulations. It supports a stage-conditioned response and leaves reserve flux and actual laying costs unmeasured. Treat the established-stage result as a boundary on transfer to early worker colonies. FT01 documents post-flight tissue changes in unfed foundresses. B05 concerns production of sexual females and cannot identify the energy export of a viable first-worker egg. B03's day-49 brood and worker counts after food offers do not isolate timing or the quantities assimilated by the queen. [Maternal and founding evidence](CROSS_DOMAIN_CONFLICTS.md#int-c02--foundress-care-reserves-provisioning-and-egg-production)

The archived model's unsafe post-laying exposures fell from 21 of 293 births to zero after the maintenance-horizon intervention. In the diagnostic ordinary balanced eight-worker seed 1, the original egg debit left 4.444 hours of basal energy before a six-hour absorption pulse. This establishes the mechanism of that failure and the guard's effect under that version. It supplies no measured two-day reserve threshold. In the ordinary balanced scenario, changing the guard from six hours to two days to four days changed mean lifetime and birth count jointly. For 32 workers the respective pairs were 43.005 days and 15.67 births, 72.301 days and 18.00 births, and 90.000 days and 2.50 births. Longer life in the fixture can coincide with sharply reduced reproduction. [Maternal accounts and sensitivity arms](../../S6_3_1_RESULTS.md)

The necessary endpoints are initial and remaining usable reserves, timed egg export and laying, and viable progression by stage, worker access and measured supply. Existing data constrain direction and scale under separate assays. New matched compartment and timing observations are needed. An effective reserve model remains plausible if those predictions hold without tuning an unmeasured budget to a final worker count.

## BR-04 | Active-temperature stage timing

B06 reports near-25 °C egg means of 16.7 and 15.7 d and total first-brood times of 41.1 and 39.9 d for the Borisovka and St Petersburg populations. The historical 2/3/2-day fully supplied fixture has a 2-day egg clock and a 7-day total. The egg differences are 14.7 and 13.7 d, and the empirical-to-model total ratios are approximately 5.9 and 5.7. This is an unmatched scale diagnostic. Larval time in the Core follows a protein gate, the paper combines prepupal and pupal landmarks, and the fixture has no ambient-temperature law. The header seconds-scale defaults are engineering values. At the nominal 17 °C treatment, larval diapause prevents later duration estimates. [Stage endpoints and contexts](EVIDENCE_CONFLICTS.md#b1-c05--development-time-and-diapause)

B01's 62.88 ± 2.52 d to first observed worker starts at nuptial flight and concerns 17 producing queens among 22 survivors and 24 enrolled. Pre-laying and observation cadence affect it. Existing B06 data can constrain active stage timing in defined regimes, with the published population and nesting retained. Independent matched landmarks, nutrition and active temperature contrasts are needed for a broader claim. Cold nonprogression and the absence of a thermal extrapolation must stay explicit if v1.0 is limited to measured active conditions.

## BR-05 | Actual worker activity, inactivity and energetic cost

In S6.1 supplied runs 97.5901% of living time was assigned to active programs, with 96.2299% of reconsiderations blocked by travel or cargo ownership. Open no-food cases remained about 97.70% active by assignment and sealed cases were 0%. These are program, location and eligibility statistics. There is no measured prediction error for real inactivity or sleep in those data. B3-T01 short-event and minute-scan data, B3-T05 and T06 aggregation assays and B3-T09 pooled age and role samples each provide a comparator in their own observation window. They do not give state-transition hazards, actual expenditure or a sleep mechanism. [Historical program measurements](../../S6_1_RESULTS.md) [Activity conflicts](CROSS_DOMAIN_CONFLICTS.md#int-c06--program-choice-observed-activity-and-energy-expenditure)

The exact energy unit identity is 1 nJ/ms = 1 µW. In N27's 20 °C Clark setup, a conditional oxygen-to-power calculation gives roughly 34.34 µW for queens and 5.53 µW for workers. The long-term basal fixture is 1 µW and the header default is 1,000 µW. Assay and cohort differences and the conversion assumption rule out direct replacement. A forager label and a rest-context oxygen measurement cannot isolate an activity premium. Hydration loss requires an observed flux, beyond survival under dry exposure. [Respiration and unit bounds](EVIDENCE_CONFLICTS.md#b1-c07--respiration-chemical-energy-and-the-source-cost-model)

The scoped claim needs timed actions, inactive bouts and task changes with corresponding caste, cohort, mass and conditions, plus separately constrained expenditure and water loss. A coarse state model may suffice. Physiological sleep and a general fatigue or circadian law remain optional. The observed activity and energetic constraints should hold together, including situations where role remains constant and activity changes.

## BR-06 | Discovery, local information and individual return

Direct trajectories and encounter-conditioned food assays do not give the current exact-nearby-mass observation a sensory interpretation. B2-F01 trajectories and their reuse in one B2-F03 comparison are partly the same data. A track segment or repeated ant choice is not a new colony. The accepted audit provides no matched discrepancy for the full unmarked search-to-delivery path in founding cohorts. [Local sensory and route evidence](CROSS_DOMAIN_CONFLICTS.md#int-c04--local-sensing-route-learning-and-social-recruitment)

The bounded effective rule can be retained if independent matched tests constrain unmarked discovery, travel and failures, loaded return and amount actually collected. Unseen food and blocked connectivity are information controls. The exact radius, decision weights and expiry require sensitivity or a better observable model form. They cannot be inferred from successful trips alone.

## BR-07 | Scout-to-naive recruitment

The S2.2 prepared cue results support local response within their engineering test. In the matched real-scout simulation, fresh trace produced 77/128 discoveries versus 74/128 with no cue, a +2.34 pp contrast with a seed-cluster 95% interval of [−8.59, 12.50]. Restricted discovery time was +3.40 s [−11.24, 17.86] and delivery +39.06 µg [−140.63, 218.75]. These estimates allow effects in both directions. Trace absent at 600 s leaves an identical discovery contrast. This is inconclusive evidence for a simulator-level social benefit under that protocol. [Archived recruitment and controls](../../S2_2_RESULTS.md)

P04 observed 348/398 first marked-branch choices under a different biological protocol. P03 reports a null learning benefit after cue removal. P09/B3-T04 are the same deprivation assays with scout and nestmate endpoints, and P12 did not establish tactile communication of the correct turn. P15's 47 min behavioral e-folding estimate is distinct from a chemical half-life. An effective cue model can meet the proposed scope with matched marking, cue age, naive departure, discovery and return contrasts. These require independent biological groups. Local occupancy or increased experienced return cannot clear the social claim. ## BR-08 | Bounded survival, age and life stage

B3-P01's common-garden worker cohorts had mean lifespans of 434.35 and 309.23 d for early and intermediate natal stages, with no resolved late-age hazard difference after about 400 d. The cohorts pool workers from natal colonies. B3-P12's worker overwinter effect, queen temperature null and association with four queen losses are distinct from warm active-season hazards. B3-P07 bait detection did not track individual colonies through flooding. None supports a directly comparable queen, brood and worker mortality rate for the current fixtures. [Cohort and environmental limits](CROSS_DOMAIN_CONFLICTS.md#int-c03--stage-timing-dormancy-age-and-survival)

The current deterministic depletion events and the ordinary queen losses are documented software outcomes. They cannot establish predicted survival for a viable population. The scope can defer the lifespan tail, rare events and winter. In its active window, clearance needs measured stage- and cohort-specific risk or a justified low-background-risk approximation, with known hydration, intake, developmental state and censoring. The mortality question consumes the earlier budget and development evidence. It should not duplicate the maternal provision remediation.

## BR-09 | Excavation, transport and limited 3D morphology

B3-N01's experimental groups showed resumption after worker addition. B3-N02 observed a 2D shape transition in 12/20 and 29/33 assays by group size. B3-N03's cohesive treatment differed in transition incidence and retained a conditional final-area null at p=0.090. B3-N04 above-floor pellet experiments and B3-N05 CT-derived soil and shape observations use different settings and endpoints. B3-N05 has unresolved printed dry-mass and depth statistics. The Core's local material movement and connectivity do not provide a matched initiation, transported mass or 3D shape estimate. [Excavation and material evidence](CROSS_DOMAIN_CONFLICTS.md#int-c07--material-movement-and-nest-form) [Unresolved source comparisons](CROSS_DOMAIN_CONFLICTS.md#int-c10--source-counts-units-and-unresolved-printed-statistics)

A measured fixed substrate and a simple local digging rule can be admissible when there are independent time series for excavation, transported and deposited soil, and coarse connected occupancy. Use a relevant local perturbation to distinguish initiation and continuation from geometry alone. Full underground microclimate, spontaneous refill and mature architecture remain outside the proposed minimum. No matched result presently justifies transfer to other substrates or group sizes.

## Cross-domain limits carried forward

Preserve the original B01 and B02 denominators and exclusions, B07 species- and estimand-specific tests, P15 proxy persistence, and B3-N05 statistics and restricted data status. Existing readings and reproducible descriptive checks keep these records traceable. They do not resolve mismatched measurement definitions. [Accepted source issues](CROSS_DOMAIN_CONFLICTS.md#int-c10--source-counts-units-and-unresolved-printed-statistics)

Increased delivery, larger stores, altered uptake, lower activity cost, delayed reproduction and different stage or death timing can each improve an aggregate count. More frequent sampling or longer task residence can change the measured opportunities without improving performance per act. Cue strength and route experience can also compensate in traffic totals. Required integrated predictions need the component quantities constrained together and assessed on independent biological data. The 38 presently non-identifiable parameter rules remain open. A future analysis may replace some with simpler measurable functions.
